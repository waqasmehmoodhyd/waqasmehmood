How to Do a Competitor Backlink Analysis: Finding the Best Links to Beat Your Rivals

Ever wonder why your competitor ranks above you on Google even though your website looks nicer? Most of the time, the secret is backlinks. A backlink is simply when another website links to yours, like a trusted friend recommending a great restaurant. In this simple guide, you will learn how to look at your competitor's backlinks, see where their authority comes from, and get those same great links for yourself.

By Waqas Mehmood (Senior SEO Specialist) • 12 min read

What Are Backlinks? Think of Them Like Trusted Friend Recommendations

Before you analyze your competitors, let us make sure you understand exactly what a backlink is in plain words. A backlink is simply an active link on someone else's website that points to your website. For example, if a local news website or popular blog writes an article and says: "We recommend checking out Waqas Mehmood for SEO audits", and when people click that sentence it opens waqasmehmood.com, you just earned a backlink! Why does Google care about backlinks? Think of each backlink like a vote of trust. If you are moving to a new city and want to find the best doctor, you might ask five local people. If all five people recommend the exact same clinic, you trust that recommendation. Googlebot does the exact same thing! When many trusted, respected websites link to your page, Google says: "Wow, people really trust this site! Let us move it to Page 1."

  • ✓ A backlink is a clickable link from an external website to your site.
  • ✓ Google treats high-quality backlinks as votes of trust and authority.
  • ✓ Links from relevant websites in your same industry carry the most ranking power.
  • ✓ Natural editorial links are safe and protect your site from Google penalties.
Quality is 100x More Important Than Quantity

One single recommendation from a respected university, news site, or industry leader is worth more than 500 spam links from random fake blogs. Never buy cheap link packages!

What is a "Link Gap" and Why is It the Fastest Shortcut to the Top?

Now that you know what a backlink is, here is the secret growth hack used by top SEO specialists: "The Competitor Link Gap". Instead of guessing who might want to link to your website, you simply spy on the websites that are already ranking at the very top of Google for your dream keyword. Think about it: Your competitor did years of hard work finding bloggers, news reporters, business directories, and industry partners to link to them. By doing a competitor backlink analysis, you can see their entire list in five minutes! A "Link Gap" simply means: "Websites that link to two or three of your competitors, but do not link to you yet." If a local business directory or industry blog already links to three other companies in your field, they will almost certainly be happy to link to you too if you have a great website and politely reach out to them!

  • ✓ Pick your top 2 or 3 real competitors who rank #1, #2, and #3 on Google.
  • ✓ Look for websites that link to multiple competitors at the same time.
  • ✓ Filter out low-quality spam directories and focus on real blogs and news sites.
  • ✓ Make a neat list of the best 20 websites you want to reach out to.

The 3 Easy Ways to Get Those Same Links for Your Own Website

Once you have your list of websites that link to your competitors, how do you actually get them to link to you? Here are the three most effective, friendly methods: Method 1: "The Resource Page Addition". Many industry blogs have a page called "Helpful Tools" or "Useful Resources". If your competitor is listed there, send a short, polite email to the author: "Hi! I loved your helpful resources guide. I noticed you listed [Competitor]. We recently built a free in-house tool that helps users do [Benefit]. Would you consider adding it to your list?" Many authors will happily add your link! Method 2: "The Broken Link Reclaim". Sometimes a competitor changes their website or goes out of business, leaving behind broken links on external blogs. If you find a blog linking to a dead 404 page on your competitor's site, let the editor know and offer your live, updated guide as a helpful replacement. Method 3: "Guest Contributions". If your competitor wrote an educational article for an industry magazine, you can reach out to that same magazine editor with a fresh, original topic that provides even more value to their readers.

  • ✓ Find the editor's real name or email address on their contact page.
  • ✓ Compliment a specific article they wrote so they know you are a real human.
  • ✓ Explain clearly how linking to your free tool or guide helps their readers.
  • ✓ Follow up politely once after 5 days if they did not see your first note.
Always Be Polite and Helpful

Never send robotic, automated spam emails. Write a genuine 3-sentence message showing you actually read their website and want to help their audience.

Watch Out for the Anchor Text Trap (Keep Your Links Safe and Natural)

The clickable words in a link are called "Anchor Text". For example, if the link says "click here", the anchor text is "click here". If the link says "Waqas Mehmood", the anchor text is branded. A big mistake beginners make when building links is forcing their exact keyword every single time (for example, making every link say "best cheap seo expert"). Google has a smart penalty filter called Google Penguin. If Google sees 50 links pointing to your site and all 50 links use the exact same commercial sales phrase, Google knows you are trying to manipulate the system! Natural links look varied and diverse: - Some say your business name (e.g., "Waqas Mehmood") - Some say your web address (e.g., "waqasmehmood.com") - Some say the article title (e.g., "On-Page SEO Checklist") - Only a few say the exact commercial keyword!

  • ✓ Check your anchor text distribution using our Anchor Text Analyzer.
  • ✓ Ensure your company brand name is your most common anchor text.
  • ✓ Never participate in private link networks or paid link schemes.
  • ✓ Focus on earning real editorial mentions by building genuinely helpful tools and content.
Keep Exact-Match Keywords Under 20%

Make sure at least 50% to 70% of your incoming links use your real brand name or website URL. This keeps your backlink profile 100% safe from algorithmic penalties.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many backlinks do I need to beat my competitor?

There is no magic number! If your competitor has 100 low-quality spam links, you can easily beat them with just 5 or 10 high-quality, trusted links from real industry websites.

Are nofollow links useless for SEO?

Not at all! Nofollow links (like links from Wikipedia, YouTube, or social media) bring real human visitors and show Google that your brand is active and popular across the web.

Can bad backlinks hurt my website?

Google is smart enough to ignore most random spam links automatically. But if someone deliberately spams thousands of toxic links, you can use Google's free Disavow tool to tell Google to ignore them.
