How to Find and Fix Website Crawl Errors, Broken 404 Links, and Redirect Chains: The Easy Step-by-Step Guide

Imagine a friendly mail carrier trying to deliver letters to your house, but your street sign is missing or sends them in endless circles! That is exactly what happens when Googlebot visits a website with crawl errors and broken links. In this simple guide, you will learn how to find and fix these errors so Google can smoothly find, read, and rank every page on your site.

By Waqas Mehmood (Senior SEO Specialist) • 11 min read

What Are Crawl Errors? Think of Googlebot as a Busy Mail Carrier

Before we fix anything, let us understand what a crawl error actually is. Google uses automated computer helpers called "Googlebot" (or web crawlers). Think of Googlebot like a friendly mail carrier whose job is to visit millions of houses (websites) every single day to see what is new. When Googlebot visits your website, it starts at your homepage and follows your menu links to see all your other pages, blog posts, and products. If all your links work smoothly, Googlebot smiles, notes down your content, and adds your pages to the Google search library so people can find you. However, if Googlebot clicks a link on your site and lands on a blank "404 Page Not Found" error, it hits a dead end. Even worse, if one link points to another link, which points to another link (a redirect chain), Googlebot gets dizzy, runs out of time, and leaves your website before it even sees your best articles! This problem is called a crawl error, and fixing it is the fastest way to get your pages indexed.

  • ✓ Googlebot visits your site by following the links between your pages.
  • ✓ Broken links waste Googlebot's time and create dead ends for real human visitors.
  • ✓ Redirect chains make your website feel slow and sluggish on mobile phones.
  • ✓ Cleaning up your links ensures all your important pages get discovered and ranked.
Why Fixing Crawl Errors Helps You Rank Faster

Google does not spend unlimited time on any website. If your website is clean, fast, and free of dead ends, Google visits your site more often and ranks your new posts within hours instead of weeks.

How to Fix Broken 404 Links (The Right Way vs The Wrong Way)

A 404 error simply means: "The page you are looking for does not live here anymore." This usually happens when you delete an old blog post, change a page URL without setting up a forward, or mistype a link when building your menu. Here is the common mistake most beginners make: they take every single broken 404 link on their website and redirect it to the homepage! Google calls this a "Soft 404". Imagine ordering a pair of shoes online, but the store sends you an apple instead. That is how Google feels when it clicks a link about "Red Shoes" and ends up on your generic homepage! The correct way to handle a broken link depends on the situation: If you have a similar, relevant page (for example, you deleted an old guide to "SEO Tips 2024" and wrote a brand new "SEO Tips 2026"), set up a clean 301 redirect so visitors go straight to the new guide. But if an old spam page or useless test page was deleted forever and has no replacement, let it return a clean 410 (Gone) or 404 so Google clears it out of memory.

  • ✓ Use our Broken Link Checker tool to find every dead link on your site.
  • ✓ If a replacement page exists, set up a 1-step direct 301 redirect.
  • ✓ If a deleted page has no replacement, let it return 404 or 410 cleanly.
  • ✓ Always update the link inside your website menu so visitors never click a dead link.
Never Redirect Everything to Your Homepage

Redirecting broken product links to your homepage confuses visitors and causes Google to flag Soft 404 warnings in Search Console. Always link to a direct replacement page.

How to Fix Redirect Chains (Stop Sending Visitors in Circles)

What is a redirect chain? Let us look at a simple example: Suppose in 2022 your page URL was: `http://yoursite.com/tips` In 2024, you added security and changed it to: `https://yoursite.com/tips` In 2026, you renamed the page to: `https://yoursite.com/seo-tips` Now, if an old link on the internet points to the 2022 address, the browser has to make THREE separate hops before it finally shows the page! Hop 1 goes to Hop 2, and Hop 2 goes to Hop 3. On a slow mobile internet connection, this causes a 2-second delay where the screen stays blank. Many users will give up and close the tab. Fixing a redirect chain is very easy: you simply tell the first link to jump directly to the final 2026 address in ONE single hop: Old URL -> Straight to Final URL (200 OK). No middle stops!

  • ✓ Inspect your .htaccess or redirect manager plugin for chains with 2 or more hops.
  • ✓ Point old URLs directly to the final working HTTPS address.
  • ✓ Update your internal links across your blog posts so they use the newest URL directly.
  • ✓ Check that your XML sitemap only lists 200 OK final destination pages.
The 1-Hop Rule

Every redirect on your website should go directly from Point A to Point B. Never allow Point A to go to B, then C, then D.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will having a few 404 errors hurt my Google search rankings?

A few 404 errors on old, deleted pages are totally normal and will not get your site penalized. However, if your own website has internal links pointing to 404 pages, it hurts your user experience and wastes Googlebot's time.

What is the difference between a 301 redirect and a 302 redirect?

A 301 redirect means "Moved Permanently" (forever). It tells Google to pass all ranking power to the new page. A 302 redirect means "Moved Temporarily" (just for a short time), so Google keeps the old page in memory.

How long does Google take to notice when I fix a broken link?

Usually within 3 to 14 days, depending on how often Googlebot crawls your site. You can speed this up by clicking "Validate Fix" in Google Search Console.
